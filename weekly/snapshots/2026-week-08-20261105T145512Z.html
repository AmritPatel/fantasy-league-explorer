Team ranking distance

Rankings describe improvement since 2025—not who has the best roster or is most likely to win.

2D center distance: “How different are these teams?” Measures the straight-line distance between their average player positions on the chart, considering both touches and points per touch on comparable scales. Teams can have similar ranking scores but a large distance if one improved through more touches and the other through better efficiency.

COM score gap: “How far apart are their improvement scores?” Combines changes in touches and points per touch into one score, with equal weight. Teams with similar scores appear close together. This is the clearer view of ranking separation.

Both modes keep the same ranking order; only the spacing changes. COM (center of mass) is each team’s average player position. COM score gap is the default to make ranking separation easier to compare; choose 2D center distance to explore differences in roster trends.

What the combinations can mean

Power ranks better than improvement: established strength. A team can rank highly without much year-over-year growth because already-good players remain good. Strong draft picks may contribute, but the ranks alone cannot establish draft quality or actual high performance; trades, waivers and lineup choices may also matter.

Improvement ranks better than power: momentum, not necessarily strength. Players may be rising from a low starting point without becoming elite. Injuries, positional balance and contributions outside RB/WR/TE may also help explain the difference.

High on both: strength plus momentum. A favorable projected team rank is paired with relatively strong improvement among comparable players. That is encouraging, not a guarantee of future wins.

Low on both: two reasons to review the roster. Lower projected power and weaker relative improvement may suggest areas to strengthen. It does not prove poor drafting; injuries, missing baselines and small samples can affect the picture.

Similar ranks: the two views broadly agree. That agreement can occur near the top, middle or bottom. A one-place difference should not be treated as a major signal.

Different scope: improvement equally weights comparable RB/WR/TE, including bench and IR, and excludes players without a historical baseline. Projected power is ESPN’s team-level projection—not a verified performance ranking or league standings. The snapshots cannot isolate the effect of drafting or explain ESPN’s ranking formula.
